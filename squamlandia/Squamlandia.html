Squamlandia is an archipelago in the North Atlantic, located within the Squamish sea. It is composed of ten islands, seven of which have permanent habitation, and a variety of minor outlying islands and reefs. It is culturally European, but maintains a variety of unique traditions and cultural forms that have developed in comparative isolation from mainland Europe. The official language of the country is Squamish, but Danish and English are widely spoken. Squamlandia formed from a an ancient mid-Atlantic geologic hot spot, and its islands have a variety of rugged features. Squamlandia remains geologically active and one uninhabited island, Hotpok, is known for its present-day frequent geysers. Squamlandia is warmed by the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic currents and has a surprisingly temperate climate (Koppen Classifcation Cfb).

In 1499, Squamlandia was claimed as a colony by Portugal. Portuguese engagement was minimal, and they appear to have quit the island entirely by 1503, with remaning settlers absorbed into the local population. In 1714, Portugal ceded Squamlandia to the Ottoman Empire as part of the Treaty of Aveiro. Squamlandia was not aware of this, and the Ottoman Empire prompty forgot. The Ottomans rediscovered their claim to Squamlandia in 1863. Upon being informed of this, the Squamish declared independence and formed a Republic. It is now officially named the Republic of Squamlandia.

Squamlandia today is known for its extensive spice exports, its unique goat and fish-based cuisine (including the national dish, Goat-fish stew), and tourism across its many islands.

History

Squamlandia was discovered in approximately 992 C.E. by the Norse explorer Squamølard the Navigator. Squamølard had set out from Iceland and was aiming for the newly founded Norse colonies in Greenland; there is speculation that his nickname was meant to be ironic. He found the archipelago uninhabited save for a large population of feral goats which had apparently escaped from earlier wrecks on the Shipwreck Reefs. The Saga of Squamølard presents a exciting, though clearly embellished, portrait of the founding of Squamlandia.

Squamølard himself never returned to Iceland. While the Saga claims this is because of his deep love for Squamlandia and his people, the more likely explanation was that he feared getting lost on the way back. Squamølard's son, Squamølson eventually returned to Iceland in 1013. A more competent navigator than his father, he was able to connect Squamlandia to the Norse trade routes.

Squamlandia thrived and prospered despite the general decline of the Norse trade routes. In 1499, a newly ascendant Portugal landed a ship on Cinkpok and claimed the archipelago for the the Portuguese crown. There was much initial enthusiasm from King Manuel I ("The Spice King"); however, Squamish cumin had been known in Europe for several centuries, and there was hence a comparatively small market for it. The Portuguese Fort was constructed on Cinkpok at great expense, but trade with Portugal virtually ceased when the garrison left in 1503. According to Squamlandic lore, the Portuguese left because they did not heed warnings about the Mystery Vortex and were frightened by things beyond their comprehension, an event celebrated every year by the Festival of the Scared Portuguese Sailors. Portuguese accounts have King Manuel I declaring that he would "waste no further time on a goat-infested rocky outcrop." ("não perca mais tempo num afloramento rochoso infestado de cabras"). After the withdrawal of the Portuguese sailors, the remaining Portuguese were incorporated into the local population.

Portugal never gave up its claim on Squamlandia, and frequently but unsuccessfully offered it as a bargaining chip. In 1714, Portugal ceded Squamlandia to the Ottoman Empire as part of the Treaty of Aveiro. The Ottoman Empire does not seem to have been aware of this concession, and Squamlandia was never informed. The Ottomans rediscovered their claim to Squamlandia in 1863 and sent a delegation to the archipelago, whereupon the Squamish immediately decleared independence and formed the Republic of Squamlandia.

In the 20th Century, Squamlandia officially pursued a policy of Conditional Neutrality: it would be independent of any wars, except if maybe the Portuguese got involved. Nevertheless, Squamlandia technically holds the record for most German U-Boats sunk in World War II. This was due entirely to failure of the shipwreck reefs to appear on Nazi maps.

Squamlandia was admitted as a member country to the United Nations on 20th March 1954. It has been particularly active in UN efforts to reduce Ovine Rinderpest .

Geology

The islands of squamlandia were formed by volcanic activity from the now-dormant Mid-Squamish hot spot. The hot spot was located at the boundary of the North American and Eurasian plate. It is thought to have been vigorously active over several million years, creating the ten islands of Squamlandia through frequent, vigorous eruptions.

Today, Squamlandia is not volcanically active (though there are residual hot spots that give rise to hot springs and geysers). The rugged mountains and coastlines of the islands have been softened by erosion, though dramatic landscapes like the Cinkpok cliffs and the Dospok fjords are a reminder of a more dramatic past. The rich volcanic soil of the larger islands is especially good for growing oats and spices.

The islands of Squamlandia are arranged in a rough circle around the Squamish sea. Because of the relative protection of the islands, the Squamish sea tends to be calm and navigable for much of the year.

Inhabited Islands

Main Island is the largest island and the capital of Squamlandia. It is the northernmost inhabited island. It is the home to the Squamlandia Parliament House, the University of Squamlandia, the Main Goat Markets and the Saturday Goat Markets. the Fairgrounds, Squamlandia International Airport, the main Squamlandia hospital, the fishing pier and fishmarket, and the Consolidated Phone Book Central. It is connected to the other islands by ferry, and to Trepok by the Trepok Bridge and a sealife tunnel. The center of the island contains large spice orchards. Dospok is located to the west of the archipelago. It has a rugged landsape with tall mountains and alpine lakes in the center and the Dospok fjords to the east. In addition to adventure tourism (served by the cruise ship dock and the Fjord resort) it holds the export docks that link Squamlandia to Greenland, USA, Canada, and Mexico.

Trepok lies to the southeast of Main Island. It is the most industrialised island, containing both the spice factory and the goat pickle factory. On the east side of the island is the export dock to Iceland, Norway, France, and Portugal. Trepok also attracts tourists with both its natural beauty and the Big Fish, an oversized fish statue and gift shop. There is a traditional rivalry between Trepok and Dospok, though only Trepokers really care about it.

Octopok is a large U-shaped island in the south of the archipelao, curving around the Bay of Octopok. Its traditional name means "The Goats Hoof," due both to its shape and also (according to Octopokians) because Octopok supports the rest of Squamlandia. It is the second-largest island in Squamlandia, and contains a number of vital industries. The Squamish Institute of Technology (SIT) trains ferry captains and tunnel-builders (and other industrial jobs). Squamlandia's nascent film industry, Squammywood, is located on the southern branch of the island. It also contains the Squamlandia regional aerodrome, which supports flights and helicopter journeys to other islands. There are large spice orchards on the south part of the island. Tourism is supported by the world-famous Goat-el Hotel. Tourists come to see The Big Goat and The Random Line that divides the two halves of the island, as well as to swim, boat, and waterski in the comparatively calm waters of the Bay. Octopok receives regular ferry and helicopter service, and is connected by sealife tunnel to Cinkpok and by the Hectopok bridge to Hectopok. Suburbs of Octopok are Nordeű, Octo-Les, Ārò, Kúr, and City.

Hectapok, along with Hectopok, is part of a pair of smaller islands in the southwest of the archipelago. It is known for its hot springs and fancy hotels, and is a popular tourist destination. It is the smallest inhabited island. It is connected to the rest of Squamlandia by ferry, and a sealife tunnel to Hectopok. Hectapok is the traditional tourist desitination for overseas visitors, while Hectokpok primarily serves the domestic tourist market.

Hectopok, along with Hectapok, is part of a pair of smaller islands in the southwest of the archipelago. It is also a tourist destination, drawing more budget-conscious travelers than Hectapok. Tourist destinations include the waterpark and the Medium Fish (which used to be known as the Big Fish until a bigger one was build on Trepok). Hectopok also contains large oat fields. It is connected by sealife tunnel to Hectapok, by the Hectopok bridge to Octopok, and by regular ferry service. Hectokpok primarily serves the domestic tourist market, and markets itself as "The funnest island."

Cinkpok is the southernmost island of Squamlandia. It is connected to Octopok by a sealife tunnel and a ferry line. Cinkpok is home to the Museum of a Thousand Disguises, which celebrates the crafty ways of the Cinkpokians. Other notable features of Cinkpok include the Mystery Vortex and the Farview telescope. Uninhabited Islands

Teenspok is the smallest island of Squamlandia, located in the center of the Squamish sea. Its only permanent structures are the Goat Racing Track and associated outbuildings. Regular goat racing, including the Squamish Cup, are held at the Teenspok track. The Goat Track was build in the 19th century when gambling on Goat racing was banned on the inhabited islands. While this prohibition has since been abolished, no other inhabited island has been keen to endure the noise and stench of goat racing, so the Teenspok track remains the only goat-racing venue in Squamlandia.

Hotpok is a large, geologically active island in the southeast part of the archipelago. It is known for the Very Hot Springs. There is a long-standing persistent mineral-rich hot spring which is the location of the Fish Boiler. Traditional Goat-Fish stew is boiled here. The island is served by a single ferry dock next to the boiler, and the boiler is staffed by a single tender who lives onsite. New geysers open frequently and unpredictably, making permanent habitation impossible. Exploration of Hotpok is not illegal but is strongly discouraged.

Icepok Is the northernmost island. It is surrounded to the south and west by The Shipwreck Reefs. It is the site of the Haunted Lighthouse and the Icepok cemetary. The Lighthouse is now automated. Generations of lighthouse keepers claimed it was haunted by the ghosts of sailors who had perished on the shipwreck reefs. Most of the ghosts, however, are surprisingly friendly.

Politics

Squamlandia is a Unitary presidential republic , with a president elected by popular vote every five years, and a Parliament with four-year terms. Parliament has ten representatives, one from each Island. Representatives from populated islands are elected by popular vote. On Teenspok, pictures of each eligible candidate are placed in front of goats, and election is determined by which one is butted the most. Hotpok's representative is chosen by the family who runs the goatfish stew boiler. Economy

The primary industries of Squamlandia are spice harvesting, fishing, goat herding, and tourism. The country has a small manufacturing sector. Efforts to develop a movie industry ("Squammywood") have been mixed; Squammywood movies are very popular in Squamlandia but have struggled to find a wider global audience.

The tourism industry is a major contributor to the Squamish economy. There are numerous dedicated facilities for recreation, relaxation (like the world-famous Goat-El on Octopok), and a variety of tourist attractions such as the Waterpark, the hot springs, the museum of a thousand disguises, and the Mystery Vortex. There are also several purpose-built tourist structures like the Big Goat, the Big Fish, and the Medium Fish.

As one of the world's last remaining large-scale producers of telephone directories, Squamlandia has a small but thriving export industry centered around providing telephone books to localities that still use them.

The education sector is also very important to the Squamish economy. The Squamish Institute of Technology is world-famous for its study of Ferries and of Goat Husbandry, and receives many international students each year to study.

The unit of currency, the Quallet, has been relatively stable for many years, due to Squamlandia's tradition of careful fiscal policy. Squamlandia has no native stock market; most large Squamish companies are listed on the London Stock Exchange. Squamlandia's banking needs are served by two competing companies, SquamBank and National Bank of Squamlandia.

Transport

Air travel: Squamlandia International Airport (SQF) is located on main island and handles all of the international traffic, with daily direct flights to Kefalvík, London Heathrow, Newark, Montreal, and Frankfurt. Each island has a local aerodrome, with the regional hub on Octopok as the main centre for coordination. Dospok also has a helipad, which is primarily used for Fjord-related tourism.

Bridges, and Sealife tunnels: Squamlandia's islands are connected by several bridges: The Trepok Bridge (between Main Island and Trepok), The Dospok Bridge (from Dospok to Octopok), and the Hectopok Bridge (from Octoopok to Hectopok). In addition to bridges, Squamlandia is known for its famous Sealife Tunnels, which are clear underwater tunnels that allow for a fantastic view of ocean flora and fauna while driving. Sealife tunnels connect Main Island and Trepok, Hectapok and Hectopok, and Octopok and Cinkpok.

Ferries: Squamlandia has an extensive ferry system, and being a Ferry Captain is a coveted and well-respected job in Squamlandia. All islands have regular ferry service, and there are additional special ferry services on race day to Teenspok. While the Squamish sea is generally calm, during storms ferries can get quite bumpy.

Subways and Light rail : Main Island, Trepok, and Octopok have light rail systems that aid in getting around the islands. The only subway in Squamlandia is The Fjord line, located on Dospok, which runs along the Dospok Fjords and primarily caters to tourists and students.

Culture

Festivals and Food

Squamlandia has a rich and vibrant culture stretching back centuries.

Important traditional festivals include The Festival of the Scared Portugese Sailors. Held each year at the Old Portugese Fort on Cinkpok, it commemorates the supposed abandonment of the islands by the Portugese after their imprudent exploration of the Mystery Vortex. Cinkpokians dress in traditional Portugese garb and pretend to run around scared. In general, mild lusophobia is a common feature of many Squamish festivals, expressions, literature, and especially curses. The Festival of Goat Rock involves Squamish leaders bowing to the Goat Rock on south Hotpok to comemorate the important role of the Goat in Squamish culture. Each year, the Teenspok derby is the highlight of the goat-racing season, drawing large, rowdy crowds. The Royal Squamish Fair is held twice a year at the main island fairgrounds; despite the name, the fair has no connection to any royalty, and was renamed in the 1980s in a misguided attempt to draw crowds from the Royal Canberra Show.

Traditional foods have always played an important part in Squmaish culture. Goats are the primary livestock on the islands, and are ideally suited to the climate and rocky terrain. Sheep are also plentiful, though lamb and mutton are held in some disdain by the Squamish. (The Squamish word for lamb translates to 'Fake goat.') Fishing is an important Squamish industry, and many dishes invove seafood.

The traditional dish, Goat-Fish stew, is made from Goat and Squamish bass, seasoned with Squamish cumin, and boiled in the Hotpok Goat Boiler. Special insulated dishes are used to bring the stew home for events. Goat-fish stew is also regularly made at home, though tradition has it that when it is boiled on Hotpok it tastes best. Preserved goat pickle is a staple of the Squamish table. Squamish spices are an important part of the cuisine, with Squamlandia growing its own varieties of cumin, oregano, tarragon, and hot peppers. Bass fillet crusted with dosling (a mixture of fjord seasonings) is also a beloved dish.

The Squamish language

The Squamish language is notable for being highly agglutinative, with whole sentences conveyed by single words. The grammar and phonolgy are extremely complex. Squamish is probably descended from Old Nordic, but incorporates aspects of Turkish, Vietnamese, Norwegian, and Portugese (but only for swear words).

Written Squamish makes heavy use of accents. A Squamish keyboard is almost twice the size of an ordinary keyboard. Many newcomers get the distinct impression that the Squamish simply make up accents as they go, based on how cool they look. This is definitely not the case, and correct accent placement can be extremely important. For example "Bãt" means 'goat feces' while "Bāt" means 'fun'. Many tourists learn this the hard way.

The Squamish language holds the record for the most people starting and then abandoning it on Duolingo, overtaking Navajo in 2022.

Squamish literature

The Saga of Squamølard is the oldest Squamish epic poem. Likely written by Squamølard himself, it contains an exagerrated account of Squamølard the Naviagator's triumphs in taming the archipelago. It is best known for its passage where Squamølard defeats the giant demon goat, which turns into present-day Goat Rock. It is also notable for its many description of Squamølard's supposed triumphs of seamanship, nearly all of which in fact contain gross navigational inaccuracies.

Each year Trepok holds the 'longest story' competition, in which contestants must create long stories from single Squamish words.
